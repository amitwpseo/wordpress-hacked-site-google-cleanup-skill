# Why 410, and what not to do

Goal: tell Google, clearly and for every spam URL, "this page is permanently gone", and let Google see that answer.

| Method | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **410 Gone** (+ `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`) | Explicit, permanent "removed". Search Console groups it under "Not found (404)". | **Best.** Google treats 404 and 410 very similarly; 410 is the clearer signal and often a little faster. |
| 404 Not Found | Works, but Google may keep rechecking for longer. | Acceptable fallback. Same effect, slower. |
| 301/302 redirect to the homepage or a category | Google may treat it as a soft 404, keep the URL known, and pass nothing useful. | **Avoid.** |
| Disallow in `robots.txt` | Google can't crawl the URL, so it never sees the 410. The URL can stay indexed (as a bare URL). | **Never.** |
| `noindex` meta on a live page | Works if the page still exists and Google crawls it. | Only if you must keep the page. |
| Search Console **Removals** tool | Hides the URL from results for about 6 months. Does not remove it from the index. | A temporary extra, not a fix. |
| Deleting the page and letting WordPress answer | Usually a 404 page (sometimes a 200 "soft 404" theme template). | Verify the real status code. |

## Details that matter
- **A noindex header on the 410** is harmless and a useful extra for caches and other crawlers.
- **A custom 410 page** should be tiny, with no spam and no redirect.
- **Never cache** the 410 responses (page cache, CDN), or later changes won't take effect.
- **Keep the 410 in place** until the URLs have dropped out of the index. If the rule is removed, they fall back to 404, which still works but slower.
- **Exact paths only.** A broad rule (for example a short prefix) can catch real pages. A real-page guard should always win over a rule.
- **Search pages** (`/?s=casino`) are also indexable by Google if they are linked. Return 410/noindex for spam search phrases and consider `noindex` for all search results.
